About this map

How to use
Stamp a province by tapping it on the map to open its panel, then Stamp this province. Tapping its row in the list stamps it straight away, and tapping that row again takes the stamp off. Stamped provinces turn green on the map and show a tick in the list. The count, the percentage and the island bars all follow along.
Move the map by dragging to pan, and scrolling or pinching to zoom. The + and − buttons zoom as well, and FIT returns to the whole archipelago.
Name your log with a nickname in the field under the counter. Put in Abi’s and the log is titled Abi’s PH travel log — on the page, in the browser tab, and as the heading on the saved image. The nickname travels inside the saved file too. It is kept in this browser like everything else. Leave it empty and the page goes back to being called Eighty‑Two.
Work the list by typing part of a name in Find a province to narrow it, and switching between Island and Region to group it by island group or by administrative region.
Date the trip in the panel that opens when you tap any province, stamped or not. Pick a day in Date, then say whether it is a day you have Planned or the day you Traveled. A province you have not stamped starts on Planned, so a trip can be laid out before you go. A province you have planned but not yet stamped is drawn in clay on the map, so a trip you have laid out reads at a glance against the green of the ones behind you. Stamp it and it turns green. Untick Show planned provinces in the panel to leave the plans out of the map, the list and the saved image — the dates themselves stay where they are. The date is yours to set and is separate from the stamp date, which is simply the day the stamp went on. It is saved with the log and comes back when you load one. Taking the stamp off leaves the date alone — empty the field to clear it.
Add a photo from the panel of a province you have already stamped — the photo is the one thing that waits for the stamp. One photo each, picked from this device. Provinces holding a photo carry a small dot in the list. Photos are kept in this browser and are never uploaded. They are shrunk on the way in, which also strips the location and camera data your phone attaches to a picture.
Keep a copy with the buttons at the top. Save file writes your log out as a JSON file, or a ZIP holding your photos alongside it, and Load file reads either back in. Save image draws your map as a PNG to keep or post. Clear empties the log, asking about stamps, dates and photos separately. Loading a file replaces whatever is currently stamped in this browser, so save a copy first if you want to keep it. The saved file is also how you carry your log to another device.
Where your log is kept
In this browser, on this device. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account. Stamps sit in local storage and photos in this browser's database. Clearing your browser data will erase both, and a browser short of room may discard the photos. Save file is your only real backup — use it to keep a copy and to carry your map to another device.
The count of 82
This map follows the 2023 Philippine Standard Geographic Code list of provinces, which is where the figure of 82 comes from. The number is not settled. Maguindanao was split into two provinces in 2022, and other splits and mergers are proposed regularly. Metro Manila is not a province and is not counted here.
