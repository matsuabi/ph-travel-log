How to use

Stamping
Stamp a province by tapping it on the map to open its panel, picking the day you went in Date, then Stamped. A stamp always sits on a dated day, so an empty date is asked for before the stamp goes on. Every row in the list carries the same three choices — Not visited, Planned and Stamped. A province that is already dated changes straight away; one with no date yet opens its panel to ask for the day first. Tapping the province’s name opens its panel. Stamped provinces turn green on the map and in the list. The count, the percentage and the island bars all follow along.
The map
Move the map by dragging to pan, and scrolling or pinching to zoom. The + and − buttons zoom as well, and FIT returns to the whole archipelago. A line runs from the province you have open to its panel, so you can see which one you are working on.
The list
Work the list by typing part of a name in Find a province to narrow it, and switching between Island and Region to group it by island group or by administrative region.
Fold the list away on a phone by tapping All provinces at the top of it. It starts folded on a narrow screen, with the count shown alongside, and the map takes the height the list gives up. Tap it again to bring the list back.
Your log
Name your log with a nickname in the field under the counter. Put in Abi’s and the log is titled Abi’s StampLogs — on the page, in the browser tab, and as the heading on the saved image. The nickname travels inside the saved file too. It is kept in this browser like everything else. Leave it empty and the page goes back to being called StampLogs.
Dates
Set where a province stands in the panel that opens when you tap it: Not visited, Planned or Stamped. Planned and stamped both sit on a date, so an empty date is asked for before either goes on, and the date field carries a going or went mark to say which it is. Not visited clears the province and its date again. Any photo you added is kept while a province is not visited, and comes back when you stamp it again.
Date the trip in the panel that opens when you tap any province, stamped or not. Pick a day in Date, then say whether it is a day you have Planned or a day you have Stamped. A province you have not stamped starts on Planned, so a trip can be laid out before you go, and a stamp cannot go on until a date is there to carry it. Stamped and the stamp are one and the same: setting a date to Stamped stamps the province, and taking the stamp off puts its date back to Planned — so the map never shows a day you went without a stamp on it. A province you have planned but not yet stamped is drawn in clay on the map, so a trip you have laid out reads at a glance against the green of the ones behind you. Stamp it and it turns green. Untick Show planned provinces in the sidebar to leave the plans out of the map, the list and the saved image — the dates themselves stay where they are. The date is yours to set and is separate from the stamp date, which is simply the day the stamp went on. It is saved with the log and comes back when you load one. The date survives the stamp coming off, as a plan — empty the field to clear it.
Photos
See every photo at once with Photos in the bar under the header. It lays your log out as a contact sheet, one thumbnail per province, grouped by Island, by Region or in A–Z order. Tapping a thumbnail opens that photo on its own, fitted to the width of the screen, with the province, its date and Remove photo under it. Swipe sideways to move through the rest of the sheet in the order it is grouped — the counter at the top says where you are — and close it to come back where you left off. Only provinces holding a photo appear on the sheet, and the grouping you pick is remembered.
Add a photo from the panel of a province you have already stamped — the photo is the one thing that waits for the stamp. One photo each, picked from this device, with Add photo under the empty frame. Once a photo is in, that same place offers Remove photo instead. Provinces holding a photo carry a small dot in the list. Photos are kept in this browser and are never uploaded. They are shrunk on the way in, which also strips the location and camera data your phone attaches to a picture.
Keeping a copy
Keep a copy with the buttons at the top. Save file writes your log out as a JSON file, or a ZIP holding your photos alongside it, and Load file reads either back in. Save image draws your map as a PNG to keep or post. Clear empties the log, asking about stamps, dates and photos separately. Loading a file replaces whatever is currently stamped in this browser, so save a copy first if you want to keep it. The saved file is also how you carry your log to another device.
On a phone
Tools at the top right holds those buttons — Save file, Save image, Load file, Clear and About — along with this page, so the header stays a single row. The menu closes when you pick something, tap away from it, or press Escape.
The map is fitted into the band above an open province panel, so the panel never covers the province you are looking at. The panel itself is a sheet on the bottom edge of the map, with the status and the date held at the top of it and the photo scrolling underneath, and the zoom buttons sit just above it. Close the panel and the map settles back into the whole space.

About this map

Where your log is kept
In this browser, on this device. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account. Stamps sit in local storage and photos in this browser's database. Clearing your browser data will erase both, and a browser short of room may discard the photos. Save file is your only real backup — use it to keep a copy and to carry your map to another device.
The count of 82
This map follows the 2023 Philippine Standard Geographic Code list of provinces, which is where the figure of 82 comes from. The number is not settled. Maguindanao was split into two provinces in 2022, and other splits and mergers are proposed regularly. Metro Manila is drawn on the map, hatched, so the capital is not a hole in Luzon — but it is a region, not a province, and is not counted here.
Boundary data
Province boundaries from faeldon/philippines-json-maps, used under the MIT License. The Metro Manila outline is derived from the same data — the gap the 82 provinces leave around the capital, closed along Manila Bay and Laguna de Bay. Simplified to roughly one kilometre of detail so the map loads instantly. Coastlines are approximate and small islets are omitted. Not for navigation, legal, or any official purpose.
