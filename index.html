Guide

Stamp a province
Tap a province on the map, pick the day in Date, then choose Stamped for a place you have been or Planned for a trip ahead. Not yet clears it. Stamped provinces turn green and count toward your total; planned ones turn clay. Both need a date first.
Views
Map — drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom. FIT shows the whole country.
List — every province with its status and date. Search by name, or group by island or region. Tick the square to stamp; tap a name to open its panel.
Photos — all your photos on one sheet. Tap one to view it, swipe for the next.
Calendar — each trip on its day. Tap a day or a month to see which provinces.
Add a photo
Open a stamped province and tap Add photo. One photo per province. Photos stay on this device and are never uploaded.
Make it yours
Tap the counter on the map to open your stats. Add a Nickname to title your log — Abi’s makes it Abi’s StampLogs — or untick Show planned provinces to hide your plans.
Keep a copy
Save file backs up your log, photos included. Load file brings it back, on this device or another. Save image makes a picture of your map to share. Clear starts over. Loading a file replaces your current log, so save first.
On a phone
The top buttons live in Tools at the top right, and the views sit in the bar along the bottom.

About this map

Where your log is kept
In this browser, on this device. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account. Stamps, dates and your nickname sit in local storage, and photos in this browser's database. Clearing your browser data will erase both, and a browser short of room may discard the photos.
Photos are shrunk when you add them, which also strips the location and camera data your phone attaches to a picture.
Save file is your only real backup. It writes a JSON file, or a ZIP with your photos alongside it, and carries your nickname too. Load that file on another device to carry your map there.
The count of 82
This map follows the 2023 Philippine Standard Geographic Code list of provinces, which is where the figure of 82 comes from. The number is not settled. Maguindanao was split into two provinces in 2022, and other splits and mergers are proposed regularly. Metro Manila is drawn on the map, hatched, so the capital is not a hole in Luzon — but it is a region, not a province, and is not counted here.
Boundary data
Province boundaries from faeldon/philippines-json-maps, used under the MIT License. The Metro Manila outline is derived from the same data — the gap the 82 provinces leave around the capital, closed along Manila Bay and Laguna de Bay. Simplified to roughly one kilometre of detail so the map loads instantly. Coastlines are approximate and small islets are omitted. Not for navigation, legal, or any official purpose.
Boundaries shown here are illustrative and reflect the source data as published. They do not represent any position on territorial or maritime claims, and no maritime boundaries are shown. Where a boundary is disputed or has recently changed, this map may not reflect the current or correct line.
